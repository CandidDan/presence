Presence would give people a shareable introduction to themselves: interests, projects and whatever they want others to know. They would choose separately whether to be discoverable nearby.

Nearby discovery is still being investigated. This example shows the idea, not a working detection system or a trial result.

We’re looking for places that would help us find out whether this is useful.

The host’s part

What we’d ask
of your space

Introduce the idea to visitors. Tell us what feels useful, awkward or easy to ignore.

Your part

  • Choose a natural place to introduce Presence—a website link, a QR code or a short mention.
  • Let visitors decide whether to take part. Quiet visits should stay comfortable too.
  • Share what you notice and what your visitors tell you.

What we would agree with you

  • How to introduce Presence and explain the choices clearly.
  • Who helps during the trial, what feedback is useful and how to stop.
  • Any equipment and setup before you decide to take part.

You wouldn’t need to organise events or offer discounts. The aim is to see whether a little context helps newcomers feel welcome and regulars find a reason to talk. That benefit still needs testing.

A few starting points

Topics give visitors something concrete to notice and ask about.

A separate choice

Sharing this page would not switch on anyone’s nearby visibility. Local discovery would be optional and off by default.

What still needs working out

Automatic discovery without repeated check-ins is the ambition. Small Bluetooth beacons are one approach being investigated; hardware requirements, costs and accuracy are not confirmed.

Any equipment would be intended to be small and discreet. Our proposal is for Presence to supply it for early trials rather than ask hosts to buy a beacon. Supply and trial terms are not yet agreed.

The example above does not create a live venue page. Interest would not create a public listing.
